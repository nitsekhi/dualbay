What this prototype shows
A working mock-up of the digital twin: one day of the bays as the twin would report it. Every number here is generated by simple formulas that imitate the seasonal cycle; the data structure is the one the real tables will fill.
  • Sea surface — chlorophyll-a from PACE on the chosen day (colour); pixels under cloud or failed flags are grey.
  • Transparency of the surface — the twin’s confidence: faded pixels are where the retrieval is uncertain or the water type was not seen in training.
  • Purple columns — bottom water below 3 mg/L oxygen; taller = larger deficit × depth. Not visible from space: in the twin this comes from the bay model.
  • Station columns — monitoring posts; height = chlorophyll measured at the post, so a mismatch with the surface colour is a satellite error you can see.
  • Sea floor — bathymetry (switch the surface off to see it); deep, stratified basins are where hypoxia sits.
  • Time bar — play the year to watch the disturbance → response → recovery cycle.
